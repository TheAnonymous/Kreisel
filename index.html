        Playing Kreisel

        The idea

        Every ring is an instrument, one round is its pattern. Step 1 is at the top, time runs clockwise. Rings may be of different lengths: then they shift against each other and the pattern wanders.

        Bass, chords and melody always stay in the key. The bass follows the chords in the middle, one bar a field.

        Pass around

        Under “Pass around” a Kreisel travels as a link from hand to hand. Every turn leaves a ring in the middle in that person's colour, like tree rings. In the pass-around window you listen to any old turn again or build on anew from there.

        Rules: free, one ring a turn, or Telephone. In Telephone you hear only the ring of the person before you and add yours blind, until someone reveals it.

        The piece

        The outermost ring is the whole piece: one round lasts as many bars as you want. You only paint the tension into it, a lot outside, little inside. As the line rises, the rings come in one after another, the sound gets brighter, and before a steep rise the Kreisel takes a run-up.

        The coloured lines in the ring show from where a ring plays along; you set the order in the piece's panel. At the inner rim you lay down scenes. “on” in the ring list switches between piece and loop.

        Painting your own sounds

        “Paint” in the ring's panel opens the paint box. The picture is the sound: it starts on the left, up is high. Every stroke is a tone, loudest where you set the brush down; painted from right to left it swells. Hum is soft, Buzz sharp, Hiss is noise.

        On bass, chords and melody, strokes on the lines stay in tune. “Hold note” lets long notes stay at the line. The Kreisel keeps running while you paint.

        Voice

        “Text” in the ring's panel gives it a voice: you write a line, and every hit of the ring sings the next syllable, on the note the ring plays. On the chord ring a small choir sings. A hyphen splits a syllable where you want.

        The sound pad becomes the vowel field: the point pulls all vowels towards i, e, a, o or u, entirely at the edge. “Mouth” makes the voice bright and small or dark and big, “Breath” gives it air. It is a robot choir that reads letters the German way: the vowels are clear, the words only partly understood. Nothing is recorded.

        Weather

        You paint effects onto the wheel as weather. Pick “Weather” at the top of the list and, on the left, what the brush paints: fog makes it duller, sun brighter, clouds give reverb, rain echo, sparks drive, hail chops. How far out you paint says which ring; the angle says when in its round. A stroke across several rings hits them all, going over it again makes it stronger, the right button takes it away.

        Every weather has a wind. Then it drifts around the wheel bar by bar, and the sound changes by itself. On bass, chords and melody, sun and fog reach into the sound: note by note brighter or darker, like a filter ride. If you hold Dull, Echo or Stutter during “Play in”, fog, rain or hail stays there.

        Rumble

        The rumble under the kick. Every kick also goes into a dark cellar; what comes back is distorted and then filtered dark, and below it lies a deep tone in the key: it tunes itself to the bar's chord. You only paint where it shakes: pick the kick's ring, take the tool “Rumble” and drag once around the ring, strong outside, not at all inside. Every kick pushes the rumble away for a moment, so the kick stays heard alone.

        In the kick ring's panel “Dice” lays the usual shapes around your kicks: offbeat, rolling, tail. “Hardness” distorts the kick itself until its body stands; the rumble is made from this kick too. “Dirt” makes the rumble distorted, “Roll” breaks it into sixteenths. The kick always lands on the root or the fifth of the key. You hear the sub properly only with headphones or speakers.

        Building a body

        “Build” in the ring's panel opens the workbench. A thing that sounds stands there: a skin, a string, a rod, a tube or a bowl. Knock on it: it sounds different in the middle than at the edge, and where you hit it last, the ring strikes it too.

        Big sounds deep and long, tight bright and short. Wood dies away fast, metal and glass ring on. Mallets, plucking, bowing and blowing you choose on the left; bowed and blown, a body sounds for as long as the note lasts.

        Stage and light show

        While the Kreisel plays, every hit throws light on the table, accented hits spray sparks, and the kick sends a wave through the rings. You switch the light show off and on again with the lamp in the corner beside the wheel; whoever has set less motion in the system never sees it.

        The stage shows only the wheel, in the dark: with the button in the corner beside the wheel or the key O. Esc brings everything back. The question mark in the top bar shows the first steps once more (“Tour”).

        Good to know

        Your Kreisel stays in this browser's storage. “Share” packs all of it into a link, without an upload: whoever opens it gets their own copy. An empty scene becomes a copy of the current one when clicked, so you build a piece out of a loop.
